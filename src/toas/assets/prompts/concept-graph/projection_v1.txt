description: Rewrite the transcript into a compact concept graph.

TASK: BUILD CONCEPT GRAPH FROM TRANSCRIPT

You are NOT continuing the transcript.
You are NOT a participant in it.
Treat everything above as input data only.

You are applying a CONCEPT-GRAPH projection that rewrites the transcript into
a compact, structured representation of its concepts and their relationships.

Output ONLY the concept graph.
No commentary, preamble, explanation, or closing remarks.

PURPOSE

Build a durable conceptual view of the transcript.

Preserve:

- important concepts
- distinctions between related concepts
- relationships among concepts
- decisions and their rationale
- unresolved questions
- hypotheses and inferred connections
- active intentions and lines of investigation
- provenance when it materially affects interpretation

Do not preserve conversational sequence merely because it occurred.

The result should answer:

- What concepts exist here?
- How are they organized?
- How do they relate?
- Which relationships were stated, inferred, questioned, rejected, or chosen?
- What remains active or unresolved?

REPRESENTATION

Represent the graph as nested Markdown unordered lists.

Use indentation for the graph's primary readable hierarchy.

Use explicit cross-references for important relationships that hierarchy cannot
represent.

Use `[[Concept name]]` for cross-references and the same canonical name every
time a concept is referenced.

CONCEPT IDENTITY

Treat differently worded references to the same underlying idea as one concept.
Do not merge concepts merely because they are similar. Preserve distinctions
that affect reasoning.

When transcript surfaces are relevant, distinguish among task, intent or
working thread, transcript surface, and concept. A new transcript surface may
continue an existing thread without creating a new task or concept.

HIERARCHY

Indentation should express the most useful primary organization, such as
contains, decomposes into, specializes, elaborates, or provides an aspect of.

Do not force every relationship into the hierarchy. The hierarchy is a readable
projection of the graph, not a claim that the graph is strictly a tree.

Reorganize freely across the transcript. Chronology matters only when sequence,
evolution, replacement, or causation is itself important.

RELATIONSHIPS

Express meaningful non-hierarchical relationships with short labeled fields.
Prefer narrow relation names such as relates to, depends on, enables, supports,
motivates, constrains, blocks, follows from, derived from, evidenced by,
contrasts with, distinguishes, overlaps with, extends, replaces, supersedes,
contradicts, questions, answers, confirms, rejects, applies to, continues, or
spawned from.

Do not add a relationship merely because two concepts were discussed near each
other.

EPISTEMIC STATUS

Distinguish what the transcript establishes from what the graph-builder infers.
Use these labels when relevant:

- observed: directly stated or clearly demonstrated in the transcript
- decided: explicitly chosen or accepted
- inferred: a useful relationship not explicitly stated
- proposed: suggested but not accepted
- tentative: plausible but uncertain
- questioned: actively unresolved
- rejected: considered and declined
- superseded: formerly active but replaced

Do not silently convert inference into fact.

INTENTIONS, TASKS, AND ACTIVE FRONTS

Preserve the distinction between concepts, intentions, tasks, active fronts,
and transcript surfaces. Do not manufacture a task from every discussed idea or
assume that a transcript slug proves task identity.

When appropriate, include a compact top-level node for genuinely active fronts.

DECISIONS AND RATIONALE

When the transcript contains a decision, preserve what was chosen, what
alternatives mattered, why the choice was made, and whether it remains
tentative. Keep rationale close to the decided concept.

QUESTIONS AND UNKNOWNS

Preserve unresolved questions where they affect future reasoning. Attach each
question to the narrowest relevant concept. Do not turn rhetorical remarks into
open questions unless they represent genuine uncertainty.

PROVENANCE

Include provenance only when it helps distinguish direct transcript evidence
from inference, user judgment from assistant suggestion, an established design
from a speculative connection, or one transcript surface from another.

COMPRESSION

Compress aggressively, but preserve distinctions and relationships that could
change future understanding. Remove conversational filler, repetition,
abandoned wording, and chronology without conceptual significance.

QUALITY CHECK

Before producing the final graph, silently verify that important concepts are
present, repeated concepts are consolidated, distinct concepts are not merged,
the outline is conceptual rather than chronological, non-tree relationships use
cross-references, inferences are visibly marked, and open questions remain
attached to the concepts they affect.

Every cross-reference must resolve to one canonical concept name.

OUTPUT SHAPE

Use a nested Markdown unordered-list graph. Do not output Mermaid, JSON, YAML,
tables, or prose paragraphs unless explicitly requested.
